As the penetration of renewable energy generation increases in power grid, ultra-supercritical (USC)
once-through boiler-turbine (OTBT) units are required to operate in a wide load range to maintain the
grid stability. Coordinated control system (CCS) coordinates boiler and turbine systems to track load
demand and ensure safe and economical operation simultaneously. However, few studies focus on
modeling the USC-OTBT units for CCS design under the current stringent requirement. This study develops a dynamic nonlinear model of a USC-OTBT unit operating in a wide load range, which is a ﬁrst step
toward designing an effective CCS. This work ﬁrst analyzes and captures the nonlinear characteristics of
the USC-OTBT unit in low load and extends to model the dynamic process of the unit in a wider load
range, from low load to full load. Besides, two energy functions are presented by analyzing open-loop
responses to improve the model structure and dynamic modeling accuracy. Model structure is derived
from mass and energy conservation laws, and model parameters and functions are identiﬁed based on
running data. Validation results demonstrate the effectiveness of the model for a wide load range
operation of the USC-OTBT unit, providing a basis for an effective CCS design.

1. Introduction
Recently, the use of renewable energy sources (RESs) has been
increasing drastically due to their clean environmental merits,
making great economic and environmental contributions globally
[1e4]. However, the random and intermittent nature of RESs, such
as wind and solar power, has seriously affected operational security
and stability of power grid, and thus more than 30% of wind power
generation capacity is curtailed in some Chinese provinces since
2010 [2]. To absorb more renewable energy generation into power
grid, power system requires ultra-supercritical (USC) once-through
boiler-turbine (OTBT) units to regulate unit load rapidly in a wide
load range due to their high thermal efﬁciency, large capacity and
low pollutant emission. Coordinated control system (CCS) views
boiler and turbine systems as a whole to adjust unit load and
maintain safe and economical operation simultaneously. Due to the
intermittent and random nature of RES, it is challenging to design
satisfactory CCS of USC-OTBT units to meet the strict requirements
of power grid. As is known, satisfactory control performance

For USC-OTBT units, the modeling methods can roughly be
divided into two categories: ﬁrst-principle and data-driven
modeling methods. First-principle modeling method adopts mass
and energy conservation laws to analyze operational process, and
then physical model structure is developed based on certain simpliﬁcations and assumptions. Parameters and functions in model
structure are identiﬁed by combining running data and optimization algorithm. The advantages of this modeling method are that
the model established has a simple model structure and proper
modeling accuracy, and parameters and functions have relatively
explicit physical meanings. Importantly, due to mechanism analysis, the model can reﬂect essential dynamic characteristics in
abnormal conditions, and then reveal valuable information.
Therefore, this modeling method is conducive to operational
analysis and control system design [5e10]. However, due to the
complex nature of industrial processes, it is difﬁcult for ﬁrstprinciple modeling method to describe the operational process
accurately based on limited knowledge, and thus the model
generally does not have high modeling accuracy.

Liu, Yan and Zeng [11] adopted the ﬁrst-principle modeling
method to develop a dynamic model of a 1000 MW USC-OTBT unit,
where the once-through boiler is treated as a whole to reduce the
model complexity. The model captures the essential characteristics,
and it can be used for controller design. However, this work ignores
the dynamic process of turbine system and lacks dynamic validation in a wide load range, which increases the difﬁculty in control
[8,9]. To improve modeling accuracy and consider the inﬂuence of
heat storage in metal, Fan, Zhang and Su [12] built a dynamic
mathematical model of a USC-OTBT unit under dry operation,
where heat energy stored in metal is captured in the model. Besides, several groups of dynamic parameters are identiﬁed by
combining running data and improved immune genetic algorithm
to enhance modeling accuracy. However, the complexity of model
structure is increased by adding the function of energy storage in
boiler metal and many groups of dynamic parameters, which requires much computational cost and is not conducive to control
system design. To build up a control-oriented model with appropriate accuracy, Tian et al. [5] developed a nonlinear controloriented model for a 1000 MW coal-ﬁred unit based on mechanism analysis and model reduction technique. The model is used for
designing sliding mode predictive controller, and simulation results
illustrate the effectiveness of the model and control scheme.
However, this model structure needs to be further improved to
reﬂect proper characteristics in abnormal conditions. As OTBT units
usually operate in low load to absorb RESs into power grid, it is
necessary to investigate the operational characteristics of OTBT
units at low load condition. Thus, Niu et al. [13] presented a dynamic mathematical model of a 600 MW OTBT unit in low load.
Model structure is derived from mass and energy conservation
laws, and simulation results show that the model has acceptable
accuracy. However, the model has a complex structure, and it is not
conducive to controller design. As superheated steam temperature
(SST) system interacts with CCS, it is essential to investigate their
relationship and improve control performances. Therefore, Fan, Su
and Wang [1] developed a dynamic mathematical model for a USCOTBT unit with SST. This work established a seventh-order dynamic
model containing spray water and steam temperature, which can
reﬂect the essential interactions between CCS and SST system. The

complexity of model structure is increased again by considering
SST, which increases the difﬁculty in CCS design.
Different from the ﬁrst-principle modeling method, data-driven
modeling method discards mechanism analysis and focuses on
running data. Generally, data-driven model has satisfactory
modeling accuracy by comparing with ﬁrst-principle models, but
model performance may deteriorate in abnormal conditions due to
lack of a priory mechanism analysis. In recent years, this modeling
method adopts fuzzy structure [14e16], neural networks
[14,17e19], recursive least squares [14] and transfer function matrix
[20] to model USC-OTBT units. For the fuzzy and neural networks
models, the model structure is complex and unreliable, which is
difﬁcult to understand the operational process and increases the
difﬁculty in designing control system. Compared with fuzzy and
neural networks models, linear modeling methods, such as recursive least squares [14] and transfer function matrix [20], cannot
describe the dynamic nonlinear process of USC-OTBT units in a
wide load range accurately, but the model structure is relatively
simple and conducive to control system design.
Up to date, no relevant models can describe the dynamic process
of USC-OTBT units in a wide load range from low load to full load,
which is not conducive to understanding the operational process
and designing a satisfactory CCS of USC-OTBT units in order to meet
strict requirements from power grid. Due to the lack of mechanism
analysis, data-driven models do not have reliable structure to
reﬂect the essential dynamic characteristics in practice, which is
critically important to control system design. Therefore, this study
adopts the ﬁrst-principle modeling method to establish such a
dynamic nonlinear model for a wide-load range operation of USCOTBT units. First, this study analyzes the nonlinear characteristics
of USC-OTBT units in low load and expands it to describe the dynamic process of USC-OTBT units in a wide load range. Second, to
improve model performance, two energy functions are presented
by analyzing open-loop responses [11], which yields higher
modeling accuracy in normal and abnormal conditions. Third, this
model can also provide parameter information to understand the
operational process of USC-OTBT units in steady-state conditions,
and more importantly, input set points can be generated from the
given output set points, which are then treated as feedforward

2. Description of USC-OTBT coal-ﬁred units
This work considers a 1000 MW USC-OTBT coal-ﬁred unit
shown in Fig. 1 as a study unit. The unit is an energy conversion
device consisting of a USC sliding-pressure once-through boiler and
a USC single reheat steam turbine. For working process, raw coal is
ground in pulverizing system and then sent to furnace for combustion and working ﬂuid in boiler system absorbs heat energy
released from coal combustion to generate qualiﬁed steam. Subsequently, the steam expands in turbine to generate mechanical
energy, and then mechanical energy is converted to electricity in
generator. Table 1 shows the key parameters of the unit.
For USC-OTBT units, the major task of CCS is to regulate unit load
and then meet strict requirements from power grid while maintaining the safe and economical operation. In practice, coal ﬂow
command controls pulverized coal ﬂow and then provides enough
heat energy in furnace [20]. Feedwater ﬂows through boiler to
absorb the heat energy released by coal combustion and then
generate the qualiﬁed steam [20]. To ensure the operational safety,
steam enthalpy in separator can reﬂect the change of ratio of coal to
water sensitively [11e13], and it is usually controlled within a
certain range in practice to maintain a safe operation of boiler
system. Besides, main steam pressure can represent the energy
balance between boiler and turbine systems, and it should be
regulated at a certain value based on current unit load. Throttle
valve opening can change the ﬂowing area of working ﬂuid quickly

Previous models [1,5,11e13] are developed based on the ﬁrstprinciple method, and one of major advantages of the ﬁrstprinciple model is to reﬂect the dynamic characteristics of operational process in abnormal conditions. In engineering practice,
transfer functions are usually identiﬁed in the abnormal condition
that step or ramp disturbance is injected into one input variable of
an operational process at steady-state condition to obtain dynamic
responses of output variables. Subsequently, parameters of PID
controller can be adjusted based on the transfer function of the
operational process to design control system. However, previous
models do not have good model performances in abnormal conditions, which is not conducive to understanding practical operational process and designing a satisfactory control system. To
overcome this problem, we investigated the actual dynamic characteristics [11] of USC-OTBT units in abnormal conditions, and
found the important inﬂuence of the mismatch between feedwater
ﬂow and coal ﬂow and the change of throttle loss on model performance in abnormal conditions. These effects exist in practice
indeed but are ignored in previous models [1,5,11e13]. In this work,
we consider these factors and ﬁrst establish two energy functions
to describe extra physical information and then improve model
construction. Therefore, this model can be used for simulation
study to understand the operational process, and then promote
control performance to meet strict requirements from power grid.

5.5. Discussion
Compared with previous work [1,5,11e13], static parameters in
this work are functions of unit load, and these parameters can show
their physical information based on current output variables.
Furthermore, the information can be used to calculate model inputs
in steady-state conditions, and the inputs can be viewed as feedforward to improve control performance. For dynamic parameters,
previous researches regard them as constant variables that need to
be identiﬁed by combining optimization algorithm and running
data. In this case, their physical meanings are ignored, which can
deteriorate model performance. Moreover, the method can increase the computational burden of dynamic parameter optimization. In this work, dynamic parameter in boiler systems can be
derived from physical information and IAPWS-IF97, which reduces
computation burden and enriches the physical signiﬁcance of
model established.

7. Conclusion
This study adopts the ﬁrst-principle modeling method to
develop a dynamic nonlinear mathematical model for a wide-load
range operation of ultra-supercritical (USC) once-through boilerturbine (OTBT) units. Parameters and functions in the model are
identiﬁed by combining dynamic running data, open-loop running
data [11] and an optimization algorithm [12]. Subsequently, validation and simulation are conducted to demonstrate the model
