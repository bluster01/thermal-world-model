It is challenging and interesting to establish a precise dynamic model of an OTB (once-through boiler)
power plant unit in order to meet large scale load demands from the power grid. This study proposes
to establish such a dynamic mathematical model of an ultra-supercritical OTB unit under dry operating
conditions. More precisely, the dynamic model structure was derived from mass and energy conservation
laws as well as thermodynamic principles under some reasonable simplifications and assumptions. Then
an IGA (immune genetic algorithm) was improved to identify the parameters, combined with running
data. After this, to further enhance model performance, the dynamic mathematical model was extended
to be the one with different sets of parameters and functions under different monotonous load ranges.
Additionally, open- and closed-loop experiments were conducted in order to further validate the developed model. The experimental results show that the model outputs can approach the actual running data
over a wide operating range with appropriate accuracy. More importantly, the dynamic model captures
the essential dynamic characteristics of the unit. Therefore, the model can be feasible and applicable for
simulation analysis and testing control algorithms.

1. Introduction
Coal-fired power plants play a highly significant role in Chinese
power generation, even though renewable energy will be rapidly
developed in recent years [1]. Meanwhile, it is estimated that the
consumption of China’s primary energy will reach 4.8 billion tons
of standard coal in 2020, and the fossil fuel can account for 70%
of the power demand [2]. In the process of coal burning, many
air pollutants may be released, namely, sulfur dioxide (SO2), nitrogen oxides (NOx), and inhalable particle whose diameter is less
than 10 lm (PM10), extremely dangerous to human health and
the global climate [3–7]. Due to the huge environmental pollution
caused by coal burning in every year, Chinese government promises to reduce the CO2 emission per unit of GDP by 60–65% in
2030, compared with that of 2005 [2]. Therefore, it is a pressing
need for coal fired power plants in China to find an effective solution to meet above requirements.
Ultra-supercritical coal fired technology is a considerable
option, and owns high operating efficiency and low CO2 emissions
per megawatt (MW) generation. According to the statistical data,
the operating efficiency of ultra-supercritical units is beyond 45%,
more than around 10% compared with the same level of the current
subcritical coal fired units in China. Furthermore, in terms of the
environmental protection, the release of CO2 and SO2 can be
reduced by 145 (g/kW h) and 0.4 (g/kW h), respectively [8]. In
addition, the ultra-supercritical unit has faster speed of load

Nowadays, there are more than eighty ultra-supercritical coal fired
plants serving in China, and the plants have prominently significant roles in supplying electricity and maintaining frequency stability of the power network.
Certainly, severe operating conditions have to be in company
with a series of technological bottlenecks, such as complex startup process and less operating reliability. Especially, renewable
energy (i.e., wind and solar energy.) will be recently energetically
developed due to their non-polluting and sustainable merits [2].
Nevertheless, their unstable and random features may pose a
threat to the operating stability of the power grid. Therefore, confronted with these pressures, ultra-supercritical coal fired units are
obliged to adjust unit load quickly in order to meet the real-time
power demand.
In ultra-supercritical units, the CCS (coordinated control system) is used to solve the above problems. However, for any control
systems, the satisfactory control performance is dependent on the
accuracy and validity of the process model.
In previous studies, the modelling methods can be roughly
summarized into three categories. The first is mechanism modelling, which is developed based on fundamental physical laws
so as to master the dynamic behaviours of thermodynamic variables [9–16]. Due to the clear physical structure, the state variables
in model are observable. Therefore, this kind of model is also called
white box model. After obtaining the model structure, it is neces-

sary to optimize the unknown parameters by means of running
data and optimization algorithms [17–20]. Particularly, once the
mechanism model has been successfully developed, it is able to
reflect the rich dynamic behaviours correctly, regardless of various
inputs. Due to simplifications and few uncertainties, the accuracy
of the white box model is not satisfactory enough. Liu et al. [14]
developed an ultra-supercritical once-through unit model with
simple structure in mechanism modelling method, in order to
meet the requirement for designing the overall control system,
through a series of simplifications and assumptions. On the other
hand, some studies were also performed on DBT (drum boilerturbine) units [21–24]. Sunil et al. [25] presented a dynamic model
simulator of drum boilers, derived from the first principles and a
comprehensive method of combining lumped and distributed
modelling used for relevant components.
Then, the second modelling method contains the model using
fuzzy [26–28], artificial neural networks [29–32] and neural and
fuzzy networks [33–35] strategies. Due to the unclear structure,
they are also called the black box model, which can be obtained
combined with running data and training procedures. As the operational data is taken fully advantage of, the calculated values can
agree well with the measured. However, if confronted with novel
inputs, the predicting capability of the black may worsen. Liu
et al. [33] presented a model of an ultra-supercritical OTB unit,
and two different structures of neural networks (i.e., multilayer
perceptron network and neural fuzzy network) were applied to
model the unit using running data. The results showed the model
built in neural fuzzy network had the satisfactory accuracy and
performance.
The third is the grey box model [36–38], a combination of the
white and black box model. Considering the few inaccurate relations among variables, some unknown parameters are added in
model so as to further capture the unclear relations hidden in running data, and those parameters are finally identified by training
procedures like the black. De Ridder and Coomans [37] established
a grey-box compartmental model, including the heat loss, internal
heat exchange, convection and mixing dynamics. Then, the
Markov-Chain-Monte-Carlo method was used for determining
the parameters in model.
Due to the clear physical structure, the white box model is
firstly chosen in this study. After determining the model structure,
the next step is to optimize the unknown parameters.
GA (genetic algorithm) has successfully been used for parameter identification [14,39–42], but it is of the high possibility that
the solution estimated by GA converges to the local optima due
to the similarity of individuals and weak global searching ability.
IGA (immune genetic algorithm) [43,44], a combination of the
immune mechanism and genetic algorithm, can effectively enrich
the population diversity and avoid local optima. The antibodies,
similar to the chromosomes in GA, can promote or restrain themselves effectively to improve population diversity using a selection
operator based on the concentration of the antibodies. Moreover,
immune selection, immune mutation and the mechanism of the
elite reservation can strengthen the ability of global optimization.
In short, IGA is more suitable for parameter identification than GA.
Xue et al. [45] optimized the enterprise information system structure using an improved IGA based on the binary tree, the niche
algorithm and self-adaptive operators. Then the relevant results
finally were validated by experiments.
In this research, a dynamic mathematical model was first developed for an ultra-supercritical OTB unit under dry operating conditions. To our best knowledge, there has been no relevant studies
except this paper [14] in recent years. Due to its irregular structure
and the lack of closed-loop validations, this model can be further
improved. Therefore, we established the model with higher accuracy and reasonable structure based on the previous study [14].

The whole outline of the paper is presented as follows. The
lumped parameter model of the OTB unit, including the coalpulverized system and the boiler-turbine system, is developed by
means of mass and energy fundamental laws and nonlinear regression analysis in Section 2. During real operation, the parameters in
model are varied with time and operating conditions, and many
groups of parameters need to be identified. Therefore, the procedure for parameter identification based on the improved IGA is
described in Section 3. In Section 4, open-loop experiments by step
response are performed to verify the validity of model, and then
the calculated outputs are compared against operational data to
testify the accuracy. After this, a detailed discussion is presented
in Section 5, in order to analyse the reasons behind modelling
errors and comparison between this study and the previous [14].
At last, the paper is summarized in Section 6.
2. System description
In this study, a 1000 MW ultra-supercritical power plant unit
was researched. The plant adopts single-reheat turbine configuration and tower shape boiler arrangement, introduced from Alstom
Power Company. Then there are three superheaters and two reheaters in boiler. Also, there are four low pressure feed water heaters,
three high pressure feeder heaters and a deaerator in turbine system, and they can improve cycle thermal efficiency and feed water

2.1. Coal-pulverized system
The coal-pulverized system is a combination of devices and
connected tubes, including coal feeder, coal mill, and primary air
pipe. The system can transform the raw coal into the pulverized
coal, then delivers it to a furnace for suspension combustion. As
shown in Fig. 1, the coal feeder can transport the raw coal stored
in the bunker to the coal mill through the coal feeder. Then the
raw coal can be broken, ground and dried in the coal mill, and
the qualified pulverized coal is filtered by the coal powder separator in the coal mill. Lastly, it is directly transported to the furnace
for burning.

2.2. Boiler-turbine system
2.2.1. Simplifications and assumptions
In a boiler-turbine system, the pulverized coal mixed with primary air, burns fully in a furnace to heat the working fluid so as to
obtain the steam with high temperature and pressure. Therefore,
there are many components in boiler, including economizers,
superheaters and reheaters. Afterwards, the qualified steam
expands in turbine to work and then generate electricity via generator. The certain simplifications and assumptions are as follows:
1. Flue gas:
(1) Neglect the axial heat conduction between flue, tube, and
working fluid.
(2) The dynamic change of the flue gas is neglected.
(3) Heat converted to the tube by gas is proportional to the heat
generated by fuel combustion.
(4) Ignore the variation of the coal quality.
2. Economizer, waterwall, and superheater:
(1) There are uniform fluid properties in the cross section of
tube.
(2) Regard the economizer, the waterwall, the steam separator
and the superheater, as a whole heated tube with the same
volume.
(3) Total feed water flow, including spray water, enters directly
into the whole heated tube on the assumption that the
superheated steam temperature remains steady within a
safe range.

An antibody consists of s, c0, c1, c2, c3, d1, d2 in developed model.
Running data over a wide operating range was used for parameter
optimization in order to achieve a precise antibody. As shown in
Fig. 4, the optimization procedure is composed of three stages in
total. The first stage is initializing algorithm configuration. The second stage, including the three operations, i.e. immune selection,
immune crossover and immune mutation, similar to GA, called
genetic section. The third stage is the most important part during
optimization process, called immune section, including high antibody mutation and large scale mutation of optimal antibody. After
the mutation, the fitness value and concentration are calculated.
Then a certain amount of antibodies are generated from the mutation of the optimal antibody, in order to enhance the diversity of
population and global search speed. If the optimal antibody in current generation is equal to the one in former generation, the large
scale mutation of the optimal antibody will be executed. The radius
of mutation in multi-dimension space gradually increases till
escaping the premature point or meeting max iteration times.
The operator combines the merits of binary and real number coding. Finally, the new antibodies generate under the inoculation of
memory cells and the next evolution continues.

4.2.1. The analysis of open-loop characteristic
Open-loop experiments that step disturbance is acted on every
input were performed, in order to validate the developed model,
shown in Figs. 5–7.
Remark 5. Considering the fact that, the open-loop experiment is
out of the regular closed-loop operation. Therefore, the parameters
varied with the operating conditions, hfw = h (pm) and k2 = k (pm),
can be viewed as constants staying in corresponding steady
conditions.
The analysis of open-loop characteristics is described as follows.
Fig. 5 shows the responses of model under the step disturbance
acting on the coal quantity order. After a period of time delay, the
heat absorbed by steam gradually increases due to the inertia
effect. Then the steam pressure and enthalpy rise up to the corresponding level as well. Therefore, the unit power reaches up to the
higher level compared with the original level.
Fig. 6 shows the responses of model under the step disturbance
acting on the feed water flow. The steam in heated tube gets
squeezed along with step increase of feed water flow. Thus, the
more steam is pushed into turbine to work, and the unit load
increases. As the pulverized coal flow is unchanged, the load gradually declines to the original level after the squeeze. When the feed
water flow increases, the main steam pressure increases subsequently due to squeeze effect. As the volume flow reduces and
the main steam temperature drops, the main steam pressure declines to the lower level than the original level. In addition, the length
of the unsaturated section increases, and the heat absorbed by
steam decreases. Then, the steam enthalpy in the separator declines as well.
Fig. 7 shows the responses of model under the step disturbance
acting on the throttle valve opening. Firstly, the main steam pressure declines immediately resulting from abrupt change of the
opening. This leads to storage energy quickly releasing so that
the decline of the main steam pressure lessens, and the unit power

H. Fan et al. / Applied Energy 189 (2017) 654–666

increases correspondingly. As the fuel flow rate is unchanged, the
unit power recovers to the original level after the release. The main

the steam enthalpy in the separator declines slightly owing to the
mild variation of steam flow, and lastly recovers to the original
level in pace with steam flow due to the unchanged ratio of fuel
to water.
From the above analysis, the open-loop dynamic responses of
the developed model are reasonable, consistent with the practical

6. Conclusion
In this research, a dynamic mathematical model of the ultrasupercritical OTB unit was developed under the dry operating condition, derived from the mass and energy conservation laws and
thermodynamic fundamental principles through reasonable simplifications and assumptions. Afterwards, dynamic parameters
were identified, combined with the training procedure based on
the improved IGA and running data acquired from an ultrasupercritical plant unit. Through open-loop experiments and
closed-loop validations, the developed model can correctly reveal
the dynamic characteristics of the OTB unit. After simulation analysis, the structure derived is correct in nature. Then the comparison
between the models developed in this study and previous work [14]
was performed to prove the higher accuracy under same dynamic
operating conditions. Therefore, the model can be used for simulation analysis and testing control algorithms. Additionally, the training procedure for parameter identification was proposed to find an
accurate solution so that the model had the satisfactory steady and
dynamic performance. Similarly, the procedure can be applied to
identify the parameters in other models such as superheaters. In
future, the model for an ultra-supercritical OTB unit under wet or
start-up conditions, should be investigated.
